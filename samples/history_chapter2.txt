Chapter 2: The Road to Indian Independence

2.1 The Revolt of 1857
The revolt of 1857 began in Meerut in May 1857 when sepoys of the Bengal Army refused to use the new cartridges. It spread rapidly to Delhi, Kanpur and Lucknow. Although the revolt was suppressed by 1858, it ended the rule of the East India Company and brought India under direct Crown control.

2.2 The Formation of the Indian National Congress
The Indian National Congress was founded in 1885. In its early years it was dominated by moderates who petitioned the British government for gradual reform. After 1905, following the partition of Bengal, a more assertive group gained influence and demanded swaraj.

2.3 The Jallianwala Bagh Massacre
On 13 April 1919, troops under General Dyer opened fire on an unarmed gathering at Jallianwala Bagh in Amritsar. The massacre destroyed remaining faith in British justice and drew large numbers of ordinary Indians into the national movement.

2.4 The Non-Cooperation Movement
Launched in 1920 under Mahatma Gandhi, the Non-Cooperation Movement asked Indians to withdraw from British institutions, boycott foreign cloth, and surrender titles. It was suspended in 1922 after violence at Chauri Chaura.

2.5 Independence
After the Second World War, British withdrawal became inevitable. India gained independence on 15 August 1947, accompanied by the partition of the subcontinent and mass displacement.
